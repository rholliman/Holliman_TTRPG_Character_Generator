Where You Are

It's the 2080s. Magic came back into the world in 2011 — an event called the Awakening — and along with it came dragons, spirits, and metahumanity: elves, dwarves, orks, and trolls, people who were always latent in the population and suddenly weren't anymore. Around the same time, the world's biggest corporations effectively became sovereign nations. They have their own laws, their own armies, and their own city-sized arcologies, and they answer to no government. Between the corporate towers and the parts of the city the corps don't bother policing is the sprawl — where most people, metahuman or not, actually live.

Layered over all of it is the Matrix, a fully immersive global data network. Anyone with the right gear (or, for a rare few called technomancers, the right brain) can jack in and move through it like a physical space. And running through everyone's body is Essence — a measure of how intact your original, unmodified self still is. Cyberware (chrome) makes you faster, stronger, or sharper, but every piece of it costs Essence. For anyone Awakened, that matters a lot: your capacity for magic is capped by how much Essence you have left, so chrome and magic are always in tension with each other.

A shadowrunner is someone who takes jobs the corps need done but can't be caught doing themselves — corporate espionage, extraction, sabotage, retrieval. It's dangerous, it's illegal, and it pays. That's your character.

How the Dice Work

Shadowrun: Anarchy runs on dice pools. When you attempt something risky, you roll a number of six-sided dice equal to the relevant Attribute plus Skill rating. Every die that lands on a 5 or 6 counts as a hit. Your GM builds an opposing dice pool for whatever's working against you — a guard's reflexes, a locked door's complexity, a spirit's will — and rolls it too. More hits wins the exchange. (Confirm the exact hit threshold and any edition-specific wrinkles against your copy of the Anarchy rulebook — this is the standard version, but printings vary.)

Your character sheet isn't just numbers — Cues are short phrases about things your character says or does, and they're just as important as your dice pools for keeping the story moving at the table.

Attributes

Every Attribute starts at 1. You have 12 points to spread across the five of them — none can go above 6 at creation.

Points remaining: 12

Skills

Skills are what you add to an Attribute to build your dice pool — a Firearms roll uses Agility + Firearms, a Sorcery roll uses Willpower (or Logic) + Sorcery, and so on. Every skill starts at 0. You have 10 points to spread across them, none higher than 4 at creation. You don't need to put points in all of them — most runners are decent at a handful of things and rely on their team for the rest.

Points remaining: 10
