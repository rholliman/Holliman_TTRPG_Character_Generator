KIDS ON BIKES

Character Creation Terminal · CNSL 5339/6339

Your Mission

Build a character who is 13 years old — the same age you were when you were in junior high. Base them on who you actually were at that age (or who you wished you were): your interests, your fears, the way you talked to adults, what you carried in your backpack. The goal isn't a superhero — it's a believable kid on the edge of a strange small-town mystery. At 13, your character falls into the "Kid" age bracket, which grants a +1 bonus to Charm and Flight checks (double check this against your specific rulebook printing, as bonuses vary slightly by edition).

Who Are They?

A concept is your character in a few words — the "type" they'd be in a story. Some examples, pulled from shows and movies today's kids actually grew up on:
• The Skeptic Who Turns Out To Be Right (like Nancy in Stranger Things)
• The One Who Always Has A Plan (like Claudia in The Baby-Sitters Club)
• Class Clown Hiding How Scared They Are (like Percy in Percy Jackson and the Olympians)
• Quiet Kid With A Secret (like Eleven in Stranger Things)
• Overprotective Older Sibling (like Jonathan in Stranger Things)
• The New Kid Trying To Figure Out The Town's Unwritten Rules (like Enid in Wednesday)
• Rule-Follower Who Breaks One Rule For A Friend (like Mirabel in Encanto)
• Tough On The Outside, Soft With Animals (like Miguel in Coco)
• The Overachiever Who's Terrified Of Failing (like Mei in Turning Red)
• The One Who Notices Things Nobody Else Does (like Miles in Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse)

Attributes

Assign one die to each Stat: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20 — no repeats. The bigger the die, the better your character is at that Stat. When you attempt something risky tied to a Stat, you roll that Stat's die and try to beat a difficulty number set by your GM.

Reading the dice: d4 = your character is genuinely bad at this. d6 = below average. d8 = average, everyday competence. d10 = solidly good at this. d12 = excellent, a real strength. d20 = exceptional — this is what your character is known for.

Always In Your Backpack

Every kid has the stuff they never leave home without. List a few things.

Some ideas to spark thinking: a flashlight with questionable batteries · a Swiss Army knife · a walkie-talkie · comic books · baseball cards · a bag of candy · a Walkman and a mixtape · a bike repair kit · a spiral notebook and pen · a slingshot · a first aid kit · a pack of gum · something that belonged to a sibling · a lucky rock

Your Bike

Your bike's color and add-ons come with small mechanical perks. The four colors below are drawn from the Kids on Bikes rulebook — confirm the exact wording against your own copy, since printings vary slightly. If your color isn't listed, choose "Other" and ask your GM to assign a fitting bonus.

Optional add-ons (pick any that fit):

Knack

A small, personal talent — not a Stat bonus, just something that's them.

A few ideas: can name every dinosaur ever discovered · never gets lost, even in the dark · can pick almost any lock with a bobby pin · knows every back-road shortcut in town · does a spot-on impression of any teacher · always has exact change for the vending machine · swears the neighborhood dog actually listens to them · remembers song lyrics after hearing them once

Fear

Something your character is genuinely afraid of. Your GM may bring this into a scene as an obstacle — you can spend an Adversity Token to push through it in the moment.

A few ideas: the dark · deep water · small enclosed spaces · spiders and insects · being laughed at in front of everyone · losing a parent or sibling · clowns · being home alone at night · being forgotten · storms
